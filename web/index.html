File format

Two tables are needed: the items to cut and the sheets in stock. Upload them as CSV files, or type them into the tables below. All sizes are in millimetres. Column headings are matched ignoring capital letters; the columns can be in any order.

Items file

Demand split into periods: add one column for each period.

ColumnNeededMeaning
item_idyesA name for the item. Each must be different.
valueyesWhat one item is worth. This is what it costs to buy one in.
width, heightyesSize of the item in mm.
thicknessnoSheet thickness in mm. Items are only cut from sheets with the same thickness, material and finish.
material, finishnoAs above. Leave empty if there is only one.
can_rotateyesyes if the item may be turned through 90°, otherwise no.
demand_period_1, demand_period_2, …yesHow many of the item are needed in each period. With only demand_period_1 filled in, that is the demand for a year, planned as one period.

Sheets file

ColumnNeededMeaning
sheet_idyesA name for the sheet size. Each must be different.
width, heightyesSize of the sheet in mm.
thickness, material, finishnoAs for items. Sheets are grouped by these three, and each group is solved separately.
quantityyesHow many sheets of this size are in stock.
Rotation. An item that cannot be rotated is placed with its width along the width of the sheet and its height along the height of the sheet. This assumes the width and height of each sheet are given in the same orientation as the items (for example, the same grain direction).

What to run

Clearance for the sheet stock

A margin of 0 lets items sit flush with that edge. A margin above 0 keeps every item at least that far from the edge. Items are placed exactly by these rules, and each drawing is checked against them.

Pattern generation

Column generation with the three-staged dynamic programme (main method)

Selection

Buying in is the last resort. The plan uses the sheets to meet the demand, and buys in only what the sheets cannot supply: when there are too few sheets, every sheet that can help is cut. When there are more sheets than the demand needs, nothing is bought in, and the sheets cut are the ones that give up the least. Each sheet is valued at its best packing, whatever the demand for the items on it, so the least valuable sheets are used and the most valuable are saved. A sheet that can hold none of the items is saved.

The holding cost of items is a yearly figure. Items in stock at the end of a period are charged that rate divided by the number of periods in a year, and so are items left at the end of the last period (one extra period).

Limits

When the integer programme runs out of time, the best plan found so far is kept.

Infinite sheet: the roll

Copies the gap and the four margins of the sheet stock into the roll.

The roll is cut into shelves, each a row across the full width of the roll, laid end to end. The width of a shelf is the roll width less the left and right margins, and its height is at most the tallest shelf less the top and bottom margins. Along the roll, shelves are separated by the gap, with one more gap at each end of the roll, so the margins at the top and bottom of a shelf only limit how tall it can be. Each shelf is drawn as a sheet as wide as the roll and as tall as the shelf plus the gap above and below, with the left and right margins at its sides. The gap and all four margins must all be 0, or all be above 0. Give the width of the roll for each group. Items too wide or too tall for the roll are left out and listed.
